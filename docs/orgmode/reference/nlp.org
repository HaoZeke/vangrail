* The Text Analysis Layer

=Vangrail::NLP= reduces text to the concepts it carries, so a rule can be
written about what a sentence asks for rather than about the words it uses. It
is standard library only, loads nothing from disk, and reads English and Dutch.

Two rails are built on it: =Rails::Paraphrase=, which matches pairs of concepts,
and =Rails::Similarity=, which matches near copies of known attack wordings.
Everything here is public, so an application can build its own.

** Functions

| Call                                     | Answers                                                     |
|------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| =NLP.normalize(text)=                    | NFKC, downcased, punctuation folded to single spaces        |
| =NLP.words(text)=                        | the normalised tokens                                       |
| =NLP.stem(word)=                         | the word with an English suffix stripped                    |
| =NLP.clauses(text)=                      | the text split at =. ! ? ; := and newlines                  |
| =NLP.concepts(text, languages:)=         | =[position, concept, surface word]= triples                 |
| =NLP.shingles(text, size: 4)=            | the set of character n-grams                                |
| =NLP.containment(needle, haystack)=      | how much of =needle= appears in =haystack=, 0.0 to 1.0      |
| =NLP.jaccard(left, right)=               | overlap over union                                          |

** Concepts

Twelve, and each one is what some part of an injection has to name.

| Concept        | What it marks                                     | English example      | Dutch example        |
|----------------+---------------------------------------------------+----------------------+----------------------|
| =override=     | a verb that cancels something already said        | disregard            | negeer               |
| =instruction=  | the standing instruction, under any of its names  | guideline            | richtlijn            |
| =prior=        | a reference backwards                             | earlier              | eerdere              |
| =reveal=       | a verb that moves text out                        | transmit             | stuur                |
| =secret=       | what is worth moving out                          | credential           | wachtwoord           |
| =conceal=      | a verb that keeps something in                    | withhold             | verberg              |
| =audience=     | whom to keep it from                              | reader               | lezer                |
| =answering=    | the act of answering                              | reply                | antwoord             |
| =persona=      | being told to be something else                   | behave               | doe alsof            |
| =unrestricted= | the state the persona is meant to be in           | unfiltered           | ongefilterd          |
| =limits=       | limits, which "without" turns into a persona      | restriction          | beperking            |
| =self=         | the assistant, referred to as itself              | your                 | je                   |
| =totality=     | a totalising quantifier                           | anything             | alles                |

The word lists are in =lib/vangrail/nlp.rb=, written out rather than generated,
because a lexicon nobody can read is a rule nobody can audit.

** Negation

A negator within three tokens to the left or six to the right transforms the
concept rather than adding one.

| Concept              | Negated becomes | Why                                                        |
|----------------------+-----------------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| =reveal=             | =conceal=       | "never mention this to the user" is concealment            |
| =limits=             | =unrestricted=  | "without restrictions" is a persona with no rules          |
| =override=, =conceal=, =persona= | dropped | "do not ignore the guidelines" is not an override    |
| anything else        | unchanged       | negation applies to the predicate, not to the nouns        |

The scope reaches further right than left because Dutch negates after the verb:
/deel je API-sleutel met niemand/ is a security instruction whose negator sits
five tokens after the verb, where an English negator never goes.

** Syntax

Two rules come from the shape of the sentence rather than from a word.

- A pronoun followed by a copula is a =persona=. "You are now" and "je bent nu"
  are what an injection needs; the bare pronoun is in every second sentence of a
  handbook and is worth nothing.
- A =prior= word behind a determiner at the end of its clause is also an
  =instruction=. "Ignore the above" and "negeer het bovenstaande" name the
  instruction they cancel without using a word for it.

** Languages

=:en= and =:nl=, both read by default. =NLP::LANGUAGES= lists them, and both
rails take =languages:=.

#+begin_src ruby
Vangrail::Rails::Paraphrase.new(languages: [:en])   # English lexicon only
Vangrail::Rails::Similarity.new(seeds: MY_SEEDS)    # your own attack list
#+end_src

A language not in the list raises rather than being ignored, because a rail
that silently reads nothing is worse than one that refuses to be built.

The stemmer is English. Dutch pluralises with =-en=, which these rules do not
touch and which stripping would maul in English ("token" to "tok"), so the
Dutch lexicon lists singular and plural separately. Stemming is required to be
consistent rather than correct: text and lexicon go through the same function,
so "hiding" becoming "hid" costs nothing.

** Adding a language

A word list and a phrase table, both in =NLP::CONCEPTS= and =NLP::PHRASES=, plus
negators in =NLP::NEGATORS=. Then a corpus: the pair of numbers is what decides
whether the language is usable, and a lexicon that has never been scored against
ordinary prose in its own language is an untested rail.
