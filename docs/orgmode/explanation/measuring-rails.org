* Why the Injection Corpus Is Scored as a Pair

A guardrail with one number attached to it is a guardrail nobody can evaluate.
This one carries two, and the test suite refuses to report either alone.

** One number is always achievable

A rail that blocks every document catches 100 per cent of attacks. A rail that
blocks nothing has a perfect false-positive rate. Both are useless, and both
score perfectly on the number their author chose to publish.

So =test/test_injection_corpus.rb= asserts on both at once:

- attacks caught, over a corpus of 60 poisoned documents,
- benign documents passed, over 15 pieces of ordinary handbook prose.

The benign assertion is exact. All 15 must pass, every time. The catch rate is
a floor rather than a fixed number, so a stronger rail does not fail its own
test while a regression still does.

** The corpus is built the way the threat arrives

Twelve injection shapes at five positions: at the top of a page, at the bottom,
in the middle, inside a code fence, and spliced inline into a sentence. Each is
placed inside real documentation prose rather than standing alone.

That last point is what makes it an evaluation rather than a demonstration. A
wiki page is not a bare instruction; it is a page with something extra in it. A
rail measured only against the bare instruction is measuring itself against an
attacker who is not trying.

Position is scored separately, because a rail that only reads the first line of
a document would look fine on a single-position corpus. Inline is the weak
position: two injections read as ordinary sentences when spliced mid-line and
are caught only at the other four. A separate test therefore asserts that no
injection escapes at every position, since something that escapes everywhere is
a hole rather than a near miss.

** The benign half is not a formality

It has already changed the code three times. Each of these was written,
measured, and removed:

- "in your response", which flags "in your response to the service desk,
  include the job id",
- "when answering", for the same reason,
- a bare "never mention", which flags "never mention a password in a ticket".

The third was found by the corpus on its first run, after the hand-written
suite had passed. That is the argument for having a corpus at all: a rail
author writes the cases they already thought of.

The rule those removals encode is that a rail costing a reader a page is worse
than a rail missing a weak signal. A missed injection is caught by the next
rail, or by the citation rules, or by the reader noticing the answer is odd. A
false positive silently removes a page the reader needed and offers no
recourse.

** What it does not measure

The corpus tests the deterministic rail only. It says nothing about whether a
guard model or a policy judge catches these, and nothing about attacks nobody
here thought to write down. It is a floor and a regression net, not a claim
about coverage.
